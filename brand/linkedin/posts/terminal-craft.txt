I spent two days learning tmux and nvim properly. The lesson I got the most out of was the one that fell apart.

Lesson 4 was macros. It broke about a minute in, because I'd never covered mode-switching or undo. Both are more basic than anything the lesson was about. My terminal was covering up vim's mode indicator, so I couldn't even tell which mode I was in, and I hit a real "I have no idea how to undo this" moment. The fix was one keystroke. I stopped there and picked it up later with undo taught first.

I left all of that in the write-up. A curriculum that puts macros before undo has a real problem, and the only way to find it was to run the lesson live and fail at it.

Why bother in 2026, when AI writes most of the code? Because the model isn't what slows me down. Reading a 400-line diff with arrow keys is. Three agents running at once, big files, long diffs. That's terminal fluency, and the tools for it have been around for thirty years.

Ten lessons, all drilled live. The repo then ended by deleting its own configs, because they belonged in my dotfiles repo. That's the right ending for a learning project.
